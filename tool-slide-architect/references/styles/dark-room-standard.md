# dark-room-standard

Projection-first dark presentation with high contrast, controlled luminance and stage readability.

## Design aesthetic

Design for a dim room and large screen. Use deep neutral backgrounds, bright but restrained focal elements and short lines of text. Preserve technical precision without turning the deck into an engineering blueprint.

## Background

- Near-black `#080A0F` or deep graphite `#111318`.
- Section alternatives: deep navy `#0B1730`.
- Avoid large areas of pure white that create glare in a dark venue.

## Typography

- Headline: bold sans-serif, high contrast, 30–44 pt.
- Body: medium-weight sans-serif, 18–24 pt for projection.
- Labels and citations: at least 10–12 pt with contrast sufficient for the venue.
- Avoid ultra-thin weights and condensed body copy.

## Color palette

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#080A0F` | Main canvas |
| Panel | `#151922` | Secondary surfaces |
| Primary text | `#F8FAFC` | Headlines and body |
| Secondary text | `#B6C0CF` | Labels and metadata |
| Cyan | `#38BDF8` | Primary accent |
| Amber | `#FBBF24` | Attention and risk |
| Red | `#F87171` | Critical state only |

## Visual elements

- Large charts with direct labels and thicker strokes.
- Controlled pools of light behind a single hero object.
- Flat panels with subtle borders rather than heavy shadows.
- One strong image or diagram per page.

## Layout rules

- Prefer `minimal` or `balanced` density for live projection.
- Keep content inside conservative safe margins.
- Do not rely on low-opacity gray or fine grid lines.
- Keep citations, confidentiality markers and authorized brand elements visible in a consistent high-contrast zone.
- Check static readability; animation must not be required to understand the final state.

## Avoid

- White or pale blueprint backgrounds.
- Neon overload, glowing every object or cinematic decoration unrelated to content.
- Fine text, hairline charts and low-contrast footnotes.
- Removing required evidence or compliance information for dramatic effect.

## Best for

Conference talks, product launches, live demonstrations, cinematic keynotes and executive presentations in dark venues.
