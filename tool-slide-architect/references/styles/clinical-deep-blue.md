# clinical-deep-blue

Clean clinical-business style with deep-blue authority, high readability and restrained risk colors.

## Design aesthetic

Use a calm, hygienic visual system suitable for clinical governance, hospital strategy and healthcare products. Express care pathways and digital workflows clearly without treating “clinical” as decorative medical imagery.

## Background

- White `#FFFFFF` or very light slate `#F8FAFC`.
- Deep blue may be used for section pages or narrow title bands.
- Avoid low-contrast gradients and unnecessary card shadows.

## Typography

- Headline: modern sans-serif, bold, `#0A2540`; concise but not artificially truncated.
- Section label: semibold sans-serif, deep blue or cyan-blue.
- Body: readable sans-serif, preferably 16 pt or above for projection.
- Citations and notes: use the smallest size that remains legible in the target venue; never hide disclaimers or limitations with low contrast.
- Provide reliable CJK font fallback.

## Color palette

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#FFFFFF` | Main canvas |
| Primary text | `#0A2540` | Headlines and structure |
| Secondary text | `#475569` | Body and labels |
| Clinical blue | `#116DFF` | Primary accent and flow |
| Cyan | `#00A9CE` | Secondary data flow |
| Risk red | `#EF4444` | High-risk clinical or operational state |
| Warning amber | `#F59E0B` | Threshold or caution |

## Visual elements

- Patient/service pathway swimlanes with explicit roles and closure points.
- Before/after or physical/digital mappings when supported by the source.
- Directly labeled quality indicators and control points.
- Flat system cards and restrained blue connectors.
- Medical icons only when semantically accurate and visually consistent.

## Layout rules

- Use a consistent grid and stable source/footer zone.
- Keep risk colors limited to real risk or threshold semantics.
- Preserve context, population, period and metric definitions near clinical data.
- Keep citations, confidentiality markers and authorized brand elements visible.
- Real patient records, system interfaces or dashboards are optional and may be used only when authorized and appropriately deidentified; record status in Assets.

## Avoid

- Decorative organs, clinicians or devices unrelated to the message.
- Tiny gray disclaimers, unreadable references or dense walls of text.
- Presenting model performance as patient outcome without evidence.
- Using red to create anxiety when no defined risk exists.

## Best for

Hospital strategy, clinical governance, quality and safety, healthcare product presentations, medical AI and digital health executive briefings.
